Method, architecture & limitations

What this prototype demonstrates, how it is built, and what it cannot claim.

Research question

Can customer and campaign interaction patterns be turned into an interpretable lead decision system that helps decide which leads deserve continued engagement, which need a different approach, and which should be deprioritised or have follow-up stopped?

What was done

  1. Audited the original project; found duration leakage, inflated probabilities and an unjustified CNN.
  2. Compared 5 model families plus the legacy CNN with cross-validation and a held-out test set.
  3. Ablation: profile vs + campaign context vs + interaction history, in-period and out-of-time.
  4. Tested interaction effects (SHAP interaction values, additive vs full model).
  5. Separated prediction (model) from decision (explicit cost-based policy with an uncertainty-based human-review state).
  6. Back-tested the policy on held-out leads; explained every recommendation with exact TreeSHAP.

System architecture

OFFLINE (uv run leadintel-train, ~30 s) ONLINE (FastAPI, loads artifacts once at start-up) ────────────────────────────────────── ───────────────────────────────────────────────────── data/bank-full.csv Browser ──HTMX──▶ FastAPI routes ──▶ Jinja2 HTML (SVG charts) └─ validate (schema + invariants) │ └─ stratified split: 80% dev / 20% held-out ▼ └─ fit HistGradientBoosting + 15 bootstrap re-fits ScoringService ─┬─ model.predict_proba (estimate) └─ score + TreeSHAP the held-out portfolio ├─ bootstrap ensemble (uncertainty) └─ write artifacts/ (model.joblib, portfolio.csv, ├─ TreeSHAP (explanation) model_card.json) ├─ patterns (interaction label) └─ policy.decide (action + reasons) uv run leadintel-experiments → reports/experiments/*.json CRM / other systems ──▶ POST /api/v1/assess (JSON)

One Python process, no database and no queue: the portfolio is 9k rows and fits in memory. A what-if re-score takes ~25 ms. See docs/ARCHITECTURE.md and docs/ARCHITECTURE_DECISION.md for the reasoning (including why Streamlit was replaced).

Limitations (read before trusting any number)

  • No conversation data. No transcripts, messages, sentiment, objections, CRM notes or per-salesperson actions. “Interaction pattern” means contact counts, recency and past outcomes only.
  • Old, single-bank data. One Portuguese bank, 2008–2010, during the financial crisis. Not representative of other banks, countries (including India) or today.
  • Strong time drift. The subscription rate rises from about 3% to over 30% across the file. Out-of-time ROC-AUC falls to about 0.67–0.72, so periodic retraining and recalibration would be mandatory.
  • Era-proxy features. Channel, month and day partly encode the collection period. They are shown in explanations but flagged, and never presented as levers.
  • Associations, not causes. SHAP explains the model, not the world. “Fewer contacts → higher estimate” does not prove that calling less causes subscriptions.
  • Assumed economics. Costs and values on the policy page are illustrative. “Contact fatigue” is a proxy (contact count), not a measured state.
  • Outcome = one product. The label is term-deposit subscription; a real CRM would care about customer lifetime value, complaints and consent.

What a production version would need

  • Lead-level CRM event histories (every touch, channel, timestamp, agent) instead of three summary counts.
  • Conversation signals (call notes, transcripts, sentiment), each audited for leakage the way duration was.
  • A time-based validation scheme, drift monitoring and scheduled recalibration.
  • Real cost/value inputs and contact-frequency rules from the bank, plus consent and do-not-call handling.
  • Fairness review across age, job and marital groups, and model-risk governance under the relevant regulator (e.g. RBI guidance in India). This prototype makes no compliance claim.
  • Feedback capture: record what the relationship manager actually did, to measure whether recommendations help.
